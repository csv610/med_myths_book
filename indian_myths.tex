\chapter{Indian Myths}
This chapter focuses on medical beliefs particularly associated with parts of Indian society. These beliefs are not held by all Indians or Hindus; many are regional, caste- or community-specific, family traditions, or practices that predate or extend beyond Hinduism. Cultural significance and medical evidence are different questions, and respectful discussion should distinguish the two.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=0pt,labelwidth=0pt,labelsep=0pt,itemindent=0pt]
\indianentry{IND-001}{Menstruating women are physically ``impure'' and can contaminate food.}{False}{Menstruation is a normal part of the reproductive cycle. A person's menstrual status does not change the safety of food they prepare. Ordinary hygiene applies to everyone.}{ref441}{Wash hands before handling food and after changing a menstrual product. There is no medical reason to exclude someone who is menstruating from preparing meals.}
\indianentry{IND-002}{A menstruating woman touching pickles makes them spoil.}{False}{Pickles can spoil when preparation or storage allows unwanted microbes to grow. Menstruation does not change what happens when someone touches a jar or handles a utensil.}{ref441}{Follow a tested recipe and its storage instructions, and use clean utensils regardless of who serves the pickles.}
\indianentry{IND-003}{Menstruating women should not enter the kitchen because their presence affects food.}{False}{Being in a kitchen during menstruation has no effect on the food. Cramps or fatigue may make someone prefer to rest, which is a matter of comfort and choice.}{ref441}{Someone who feels well can cook. Offer help if wanted without imposing a medical restriction.}
\indianentry{IND-004}{Menstruating girls should not wash their hair or bathe.}{False}{Bathing and washing hair during a period are safe. Water touching the skin or scalp does not disrupt menstruation.}{ref441}{Use the bathing routine that feels comfortable and change menstrual products as needed.}
\indianentry{IND-005}{Menstrual blood is ``dirty blood'' or toxins being expelled from the body.}{False}{Menstrual fluid contains blood and tissue shed from the lining of the uterus. It is part of the menstrual cycle, not a process for removing toxins.}{ref441}{Handle used menstrual products hygienically and wash hands afterward. Menstruation itself requires no cleansing treatment.}
\indianentry{IND-006}{Girls should avoid curd, tamarind, lemon, or other sour foods during menstruation.}{False}{There is no medical requirement to avoid sour foods during a period. Their taste does not determine menstrual flow or whether the uterine lining sheds normally.}{ref441}{Choose foods according to appetite and individual tolerance. Persistent heavy bleeding or severe cramps deserve assessment in their own right.}
\indianentry{IND-007}{Eating papaya during pregnancy necessarily causes miscarriage.}{False}{Eating papaya does not necessarily cause miscarriage. Researchers found no maternal or fetal toxicity from ripe fruit in a rat study. Crude latex caused contractions in isolated rat uterine muscle; that result cannot quantify human risk from eating unripe fruit.}{ref276}{Distinguish ripe fruit from latex or concentrated preparations. If bleeding occurs during pregnancy, seek maternity advice without assuming a meal caused it.}
\indianentry{IND-008}{Eating saffron during pregnancy makes the baby fair.}{False}{A baby's natural skin color depends largely on inherited variation affecting melanin. Saffron's color gives no reason to expect it to lighten fetal skin, and there is no established clinical benefit for that purpose.}{ref442}{There is no need to take saffron or a concentrated saffron supplement to change a baby's complexion.}
\indianentry{IND-009}{Drinking coconut water during pregnancy makes the baby fair.}{False}{Coconut water supplies fluid and nutrients. It has no established effect on the amount or distribution of melanin in a baby's skin.}{ref442}{Choose drinks for hydration, taste, and any medical dietary needs. Complexion is not a measure of a newborn's health.}
\indianentry{IND-010}{Eating dark-colored foods during pregnancy can make the baby dark.}{False}{The color of a food does not transfer to a baby's complexion. Skin pigmentation develops through biological processes influenced by many genes.}{ref442}{There is no reason to exclude dark-colored beans, vegetables, or fruit on account of their color.}
\indianentry{IND-011}{Eating more ghee late in pregnancy lubricates the birth canal and produces an easier delivery.}{False}{Dietary ghee is digested and absorbed in the gut. It does not coat the birth canal, and extra ghee has no established benefit for easing delivery.}{ref326}{Use ghee according to dietary preference. Discuss preparation for labor with the maternity team.}
\indianentry{IND-012}{Pregnant women must remain indoors during an eclipse to prevent birth defects.}{False}{An eclipse does not cause congenital differences in babies whose mothers go outdoors. The established hazard from directly viewing a solar eclipse is injury to the viewer's eyes.}{ref443}{Normal activities can continue. Follow solar eye-safety guidance if you intend to watch an eclipse.}
\indianentry{IND-013}{Using a knife or scissors during an eclipse can cause a cleft lip or deformity in the baby.}{False}{A cleft lip develops when parts of the upper lip do not fully join early in pregnancy. Handling scissors or a knife during an eclipse has no known role in that process.}{ref444}{Use normal kitchen and tool safety. Parents need not blame a routine activity for a child's cleft.}
\indianentry{IND-014}{Cutting vegetables during an eclipse can give the baby corresponding cuts or deformities.}{False}{Cutting vegetables affects the food on the cutting board. There is no biological pathway by which the shape of a cut creates a matching injury or birth defect in a fetus.}{ref444,ref443}{Follow ordinary knife safety. An eclipse adds no medical restriction on preparing vegetables.}
\indianentry{IND-015}{A high, low, round, or pointed abdomen predicts the baby's sex.}{False}{The appearance of a pregnant abdomen varies with factors such as fetal position and the pregnant person's build. Shape is not a reliable test of fetal sex.}{ref327}{A maternity clinician assesses growth and position using examination and, when indicated, ultrasound.}
\indianentry{IND-016}{Sweet versus salty pregnancy cravings indicate whether the fetus is a boy or girl.}{False}{Food preferences and aversions can change during pregnancy. A preference for sweet or salty flavors is not a validated predictor of fetal sex.}{ref327}{Treat cravings as food preferences. Discuss cravings for nonfood substances with the maternity team.}
\indianentry{IND-017}{A fetal heart rate above or below a particular number tells whether the baby is a boy or girl.}{False}{A systematic review found that first-trimester fetal heart rate does not reliably distinguish male and female fetuses. A cutoff such as 140 beats per minute cannot tell a family the baby's sex.}{ref328}{Clinicians interpret heart rate in the context of gestational age and fetal well-being.}
\indianentry{IND-018}{Colostrum is old, dirty, or harmful milk and should be thrown away.}{False}{Colostrum is the first milk produced around birth. Its small volume, thicker texture, and often yellow color are normal. It provides nutrients and protective immune components.}{ref68}{Offer the breast early when feasible. If the baby cannot latch or the parent and baby are separated, ask for help with feeding or expressing milk.}
\indianentry{IND-019}{A newborn should receive honey before breast milk.}{False}{Honey can contain spores that cause infant botulism. A ceremonial taste carries the same concern as honey offered in food or drink.}{ref69,ref68}{Keep honey out of the mouth of any baby under twelve months. For a newborn's first feeds, use breast milk when possible or the feeding plan agreed with the care team.}
\indianentry{IND-020}{A newborn should receive ghutti as a traditional first feed.}{False}{Ghutti is a traditional preparation whose ingredients vary. It has no established role as a newborn's first feed and can displace colostrum. Preparations containing honey carry an additional botulism risk.}{ref68,ref69}{Offer milk feeds and seek feeding support if the newborn is struggling. A welcoming ceremony can take place without putting a preparation in the baby's mouth.}
\indianentry{IND-021}{Janam-ghutti cleans a newborn's stomach or intestines.}{False}{A healthy newborn passes meconium, the first stool, without an intestinal cleanser. Giving janam-ghutti has no established cleansing benefit.}{ref70,ref445}{If a newborn has not passed the first stool as expected, or has a swollen abdomen or green vomit, seek prompt medical assessment instead of giving a purge.}
\indianentry{IND-022}{Putting kajal or surma in a baby's eyes improves eyesight.}{False}{Kajal and surma do not improve a baby's eyesight. Some preparations sold under these names contain substantial amounts of lead, and applying material to the eye can irritate it.}{ref79}{Avoid using these products around an infant's eyes. If a product has been used repeatedly, tell the child's clinician so exposure can be assessed.}
\indianentry{IND-023}{Kajal protects babies from nazar and therefore prevents illness.}{False}{Kajal can have cultural significance as protection from nazar. Applying it has no demonstrated ability to prevent illness, and some products contain lead.}{ref79}{Choose a way to observe the tradition that avoids exposure to the baby's eyes and skin. Seek medical care for illness regardless of whether kajal was used.}
\indianentry{IND-024}{Applying oil into a newborn's nose and ears is necessary for health.}{False}{A healthy newborn does not need oil placed in the nose or ear canals. Liquids put into an infant's nose can enter the airway.}{ref70,ref446}{Clean the outer ear gently. Ask the child's clinician about nasal blockage, breathing difficulty, or ear discharge rather than putting oil inside.}
\indianentry{IND-025}{Forceful traditional infant massage makes bones stronger or changes body shape.}{False}{A baby's bone development is not improved by forcible stretching, squeezing, or reshaping. Gentle touch and forceful manipulation have different effects and risks.}{ref70,ref448}{If you massage your baby, support the head and limbs, use gentle pressure, and stop if the baby shows distress.}
\indianentry{IND-026}{Pressing a newborn girl's swollen breasts removes ``witch's milk.''}{False}{Temporary breast swelling and sometimes a little milky discharge can occur in newborns of either sex because of hormonal changes. Squeezing is unnecessary and can irritate the tissue or contribute to infection.}{ref447}{Leave the area alone. Redness, warmth, tenderness, pus, or fever in a newborn needs prompt medical attention.}
\indianentry{IND-027}{Turmeric, ash, ghee, or oil applied to the umbilical stump helps it heal.}{False}{A newborn's cord stump usually dries and separates on its own. Kitchen ingredients and ash can contaminate the healing tissue. Some settings use a specific chlorhexidine cord-care protocol.}{ref70,ref448}{Keep the stump clean and dry and follow local newborn-care instructions. Spreading redness, pus, poor feeding, or fever needs urgent assessment.}
\indianentry{IND-028}{A baby's head must be repeatedly shaved to make the hair grow thicker.}{False}{Shaving cuts hair above the skin and cannot increase the number of follicles. Blunt regrowth can feel coarser; an infant's hair may also change naturally with age.}{ref338}{There is no medical reason to shave repeatedly for thickness. Avoid shaving irritated skin.}
\indianentry{IND-029}{Mundan permanently improves hair density.}{False}{Mundan can be a meaningful family ceremony. Cutting the hair does not permanently alter follicle density or the thickness of hair produced by each follicle.}{ref338}{If the family chooses the ceremony, use clean equipment and take care to avoid cuts. Hair-density claims need not determine the decision.}
\indianentry{IND-030}{A black thread, black dot, or amulet medically protects a child from disease caused by nazar.}{False}{A thread, dot, or amulet can have personal or religious meaning. It has not been shown to prevent childhood disease. Cords around the neck or small detachable pieces can create physical hazards.}{ref70,ref449}{Keep symbolic objects away from the baby's neck and mouth. Continue vaccination, routine care, and timely assessment of symptoms.}
\indianentry{IND-031}{Illness following nazar lagna can be diagnosed from chillies, lemon, or salt.}{False}{Changes in chillies, lemon, or salt during a ritual are not validated tests of the cause or severity of illness. They cannot distinguish infection from other conditions.}{ref70}{Base care on the person's symptoms and examination. A reassuring ritual result should not delay assessment of serious symptoms.}
\indianentry{IND-032}{Burning chillies can determine whether someone has the evil eye.}{False}{The smell, smoke, or appearance of burning chillies cannot diagnose the evil eye or identify a medical cause of illness. Chilli smoke can itself irritate the eyes and airways.}{ref450}{Avoid exposing a sick person or infant to irritating smoke. Seek assessment for breathing difficulty or other concerning symptoms.}
\indianentry{IND-033}{Epileptic seizures are caused by possession, ghosts, or black magic.}{False}{Epileptic seizures result from abnormal electrical activity in the brain. They can affect awareness, movement, or sensation. Having a seizure is not evidence of possession.}{ref71,ref302}{Protect the person from injury and seek medical assessment for a first seizure. An accurate account of what happened helps guide diagnosis.}
\indianentry{IND-034}{Making someone having a seizure smell shoes can revive them.}{False}{The smell of a shoe does not stop a seizure. Many seizures end on their own, which can make a coincidental action appear effective.}{ref302}{Clear hazards, cushion the head, and time the seizure. Call emergency services for a first seizure, one lasting over five minutes, or breathing difficulty afterward.}
\indianentry{IND-035}{Giving an iron object or key to a person during a seizure stops it.}{False}{Placing a key or iron object in the hand does not change the brain activity causing a seizure. Forcing an object into the hand or mouth can injure the person.}{ref302}{Keep nearby objects out of the way, do not restrain the person, and turn them gently onto their side when safe. Follow any prescribed seizure action plan.}
\indianentry{IND-036}{Tantric or exorcism rituals can cure epilepsy.}{False}{Rituals have not been shown to cure epilepsy. Antiseizure medicines help many people, and other treatments are available for some whose seizures continue.}{ref71}{Respect a person's spiritual preferences while maintaining medical follow-up. Do not stop antiseizure medicine abruptly or subject someone to restraint or injury during a ritual.}
\indianentry{IND-037}{Mental illness results from possession, upari hawa, black magic, or ghosts.}{False}{Mental health symptoms have many possible causes, including psychiatric conditions, medical illness, medicines, and substance use. They do not establish possession or black magic.}{ref190}{Offer respectful support and arrange an assessment. Sudden confusion or behavior that puts someone in immediate danger needs urgent help.}
\indianentry{IND-038}{A jhaad-phoonk ritual can medically cure psychiatric disease.}{False}{A jhaad-phoonk ritual may carry spiritual meaning for a family, but it has not been established as a medical cure for psychiatric illness. Effective care depends on the symptoms and diagnosis.}{ref190}{Support voluntary, non-harmful spiritual practices if the person wishes. Avoid coercion and seek clinical care when symptoms persist or impair daily life.}
\indianentry{IND-039}{Cow urine cures cancer.}{False}{No reliable human evidence establishes that drinking cow urine shrinks tumors, improves survival, or cures cancer. A laboratory observation about a substance cannot establish benefit in patients.}{ref453}{Discuss any product being used with the oncology team and continue treatment with demonstrated benefit.}
\indianentry{IND-040}{Cow urine cures diabetes.}{False}{Cow urine has not been shown to cure diabetes or safely replace glucose-lowering treatment. Symptoms alone cannot establish whether blood glucose is controlled.}{ref81,ref271}{Follow the agreed monitoring and treatment plan. In type 1 diabetes, replacing insulin can lead to a life-threatening emergency.}
\indianentry{IND-041}{Cow urine can replace medicines because it is a natural disinfectant or medicine.}{False}{An external disinfectant claim does not establish that swallowing a substance treats an infection or chronic disease. Cow urine is not a validated substitute for prescribed medicines.}{ref453,ref81}{If considering stopping a medicine, discuss the condition, treatment goals, and alternatives with the prescriber.}
\indianentry{IND-042}{Cow dung applied to wounds promotes healing and prevents infection.}{False}{Dung can introduce microorganisms into a wound, including spores associated with tetanus. Applying it does not provide reliable wound protection.}{ref452}{Rinse contamination away with clean water. A wound exposed to dung warrants advice about cleaning, vaccination history, and whether additional tetanus prevention is needed.}
\indianentry{IND-043}{Cow dung applied to a newborn's umbilical stump helps healing.}{False}{Dung on a newborn's cord stump creates a route for contamination and is a recognized risk for neonatal tetanus. It does not promote safer healing.}{ref448,ref452}{Use the cord-care method recommended by the newborn team. If dung has already been applied, contact a health worker promptly rather than waiting for symptoms.}
\indianentry{IND-044}{Ganga water cannot contain disease-causing organisms because it is sacred or self-purifying.}{False}{Water from a sacred river can contain disease-causing organisms. Microbial water quality depends on the collection site, contamination, and treatment; appearance or religious significance cannot establish potability.}{ref451}{Use water known to be safe for drinking and food preparation. Where the supply is uncertain, follow local drinking-water treatment advice.}
\indianentry{IND-045}{Panchagavya can cure major diseases.}{False}{Panchagavya refers to preparations made from several cow-derived ingredients. There is no reliable clinical evidence that these mixtures cure major diseases as a group. Safety depends on composition and preparation.}{ref453,ref80}{Distinguish ceremonial use from a medical treatment claim. Do not delay assessment or replace effective treatment with a preparation of uncertain safety.}
\indianentry{IND-046}{Tulsi can prevent or cure virtually every infection.}{False}{Tulsi has been studied for a range of effects, but human studies do not establish that it prevents or cures infections broadly. Laboratory activity cannot establish an effective dose or outcome in a patient.}{ref454}{Tulsi tea may be a preferred beverage. A persistent or worsening infection still warrants appropriate assessment.}
\indianentry{IND-047}{Haldi doodh can cure bacterial or viral infections.}{False}{Haldi doodh is milk flavored with turmeric. A warm drink can be comforting, but that does not establish that it eliminates bacteria or viruses in a person.}{ref455,ref391}{Use it as a food if tolerated. Seek assessment for severe or persistent symptoms, and use prescribed treatment when indicated.}
\indianentry{IND-048}{Neem can cure virtually any skin disease.}{False}{Skin conditions have different causes, including allergy, infection, and inflammation. Evidence does not support neem as a general cure, and a topical product can itself irritate sensitive skin.}{ref80,ref440}{Seek assessment for a spreading, painful, or persistent rash. Stop using a preparation if it worsens irritation.}
\indianentry{IND-049}{Karela juice can cure diabetes and eliminate the need for medication.}{False}{Bitter gourd, or karela, can be eaten as a vegetable. Clinical studies have not established that its juice cures diabetes or safely replaces medication.}{ref81,ref456}{If using a concentrated preparation, tell the diabetes team and continue the agreed glucose monitoring and treatment plan.}
\indianentry{IND-050}{Jamun seeds can cure diabetes permanently.}{False}{Jamun seed preparations have not been shown to produce lasting diabetes remission in reliable human trials. A change in one glucose reading would not demonstrate a permanent cure.}{ref81,ref271}{Do not stop prescribed medicine on the strength of a product claim. Discuss repeated changes in measured glucose with the care team.}
\indianentry{IND-051}{Jaggery is sugar-free and safe in unlimited amounts for diabetes.}{False}{Jaggery is made by concentrating sugarcane or palm sap. It contains sugars that raise blood glucose. Trace minerals do not make a serving sugar-free.}{ref271,ref375}{Include jaggery when counting added sugar or carbohydrate, including the amount used in tea or sweets.}
\indianentry{IND-052}{Desi ghee can be eaten without limit because it is inherently heart-protective.}{False}{Ghee supplies energy and saturated fat. Its traditional place in cooking does not make unlimited intake protective. Replacing some saturated fat with unsaturated fat can lower LDL cholesterol.}{ref10,ref375}{Consider the portion and what it replaces. An unsaturated vegetable oil can replace some ghee in everyday cooking.}
\indianentry{IND-053}{Rock salt or sendha namak is harmless for people with hypertension.}{False}{Sendha namak is rock salt and supplies sodium. A change in crystal size or color does not remove the effect of sodium intake on blood pressure.}{ref272,ref375}{Count rock salt along with table salt, pickles, and salty packaged foods when reducing sodium.}
\indianentry{IND-054}{Black salt is essentially sodium-free.}{False}{Black salt, or kala namak, contains sodium chloride. Its sulfurous flavor distinguishes it from table salt but does not make it sodium-free.}{ref375}{Include black salt in estimates of salt intake. Check the sodium on the label when comparing products.}
\indianentry{IND-055}{Water stored in a copper vessel cures numerous diseases.}{False}{Studies of copper vessels have measured reductions in some bacteria in stored water under specific conditions. They did not test whether drinking the water cures diseases in people.}{ref457,ref451}{Use a safe drinking-water supply. A vessel's material alone cannot guarantee water quality or replace treatment for an illness.}
\indianentry{IND-056}{Water kept overnight in copper cures digestive and metabolic disease.}{False}{Leaving water in copper overnight has not been shown to treat digestive or metabolic disease. A change in the stored water is a different outcome from improved symptoms or glucose control in a patient.}{ref457,ref81}{Choose drinking water for safety and hydration. Persistent digestive symptoms or high glucose readings need their own assessment.}
\indianentry{IND-057}{Repeatedly drinking hot water “melts” body fat.}{False}{Hot water does not reach or melt fat stored in the abdomen or elsewhere. Unsweetened water can reduce energy intake when it replaces a caloric drink, regardless of its temperature.}{ref191}{Choose a comfortable drinking temperature; hotter water offers no established fat-loss advantage.}
\indianentry{IND-058}{Ajwain water burns abdominal fat.}{False}{Human evidence does not establish that ajwain (carom seed) water reduces abdominal fat. A change in bloating or fullness after a drink is different from a change in body fat.}{ref191}{If tracking a weight-management plan, judge changes over time rather than by how the abdomen feels immediately after a drink.}
\indianentry{IND-059}{Lemon-honey water detoxifies the liver.}{False}{No clinical evidence establishes that lemon-honey water removes toxins from the liver. Lemon adds flavor and honey adds sugar. A drink cannot address the many possible causes of liver injury.}{ref303,ref458}{Use it as a beverage if desired. Yellow eyes, dark urine, or unexplained illness warrant assessment rather than a cleanse.}
\indianentry{IND-060}{Eating curd at night produces harmful mucus in everyone.}{False}{Curd can leave a coating sensation in the mouth. Research on dairy intake during a cold has not found a corresponding increase in measured nasal secretions. A specific harmful nighttime effect has not been established.}{ref279}{People who tolerate curd need not avoid it at night for fear of mucus. Reflux or milk allergy is a separate consideration.}
\indianentry{IND-061}{Milk and fish together produce vitiligo.}{False}{Vitiligo develops when pigment-producing cells are lost, often through an autoimmune process. Eating fish and milk together is not an established trigger.}{ref351}{Seek assessment for new pale patches before attributing them to a meal or excluding whole food groups.}
\indianentry{IND-062}{Milk followed by sour food causes vitiligo.}{False}{Sour food eaten after milk does not remove pigment-producing cells from the skin. Curdling of milk by acid is a food process and offers no explanation for vitiligo.}{ref351}{Dietary separation of milk and sour foods has no established role in preventing vitiligo.}
\indianentry{IND-063}{Vitiligo is contagious.}{False}{Vitiligo is not an infection and cannot pass from one person to another by touch, shared meals, or living together.}{ref82}{Someone with vitiligo can participate in ordinary family, school, and social activities without precautions against transmission.}
\indianentry{IND-064}{Leprosy results from sins committed in a previous life.}{False}{Leprosy, also called Hansen disease, is a bacterial infection that can affect skin and peripheral nerves. It can be treated with multidrug therapy.}{ref74}{A pale or reddish patch with reduced sensation, or unexplained nerve weakness, deserves assessment. Early treatment can prevent disability.}
\indianentry{IND-065}{Leprosy spreads simply by touching someone once.}{False}{Leprosy typically requires prolonged close exposure to an untreated person. Casual touch, sharing a meal, or sitting beside someone is not a usual route of spread.}{ref74}{There is no medical reason to shun someone after a handshake. Household contacts should follow local advice about examination and prevention.}
\indianentry{IND-066}{Snakebite can be cured by a mantra.}{False}{A mantra cannot neutralize venom or identify which bites need antivenom. Serious effects may develop even when the bite initially looks minor.}{ref269}{Keep the person and bitten limb still and arrange urgent transport to medical care. Prayer need not delay transport.}
\indianentry{IND-067}{A snake-stone or nagmani can pull venom from a snakebite.}{False}{Placing a snake-stone or nagmani on a bite has not been shown to remove a clinically useful amount of venom. Adherence of a stone to the skin is no measure of envenoming.}{ref269}{Do not wait for a stone to fall off or change appearance. Immobilize the limb and seek emergency care.}
\indianentry{IND-068}{An ojha, bhopa, or tantrik can remove snake poison before hospital treatment.}{False}{There is no reliable evidence that a healer can extract injected venom. Without medical assessment, a bite with little or no venom can be mistaken for a successful cure.}{ref269}{Go directly to a facility that can assess and treat snakebite. A traditional practitioner can help by arranging transport promptly.}
\indianentry{IND-069}{Turmeric, lime, or herbal paste neutralizes snake venom.}{False}{Applying turmeric, lime, or an herbal paste to the surface of a bite does not neutralize venom in the tissues. Such applications can irritate the wound or interfere with examination.}{ref269}{Leave the site free of pastes, cuts, and tight bands. Keep the person still and obtain emergency care.}
\indianentry{IND-070}{A dog bite can be treated with chilli powder, turmeric, oil, or another traditional application.}{False}{Chilli, turmeric, and oil do not prevent rabies after a bite. Rabies prevention depends on prompt wound washing and assessment for vaccination and, for certain exposures, rabies immunoglobulin or another locally recommended antibody product.}{ref459}{Immediately wash the wound thoroughly with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes and seek urgent medical care, even if the wound is small.}
\indianentry{IND-071}{A person bitten by a dog should avoid particular foods while receiving rabies vaccination.}{False}{No special food restriction is needed for rabies vaccination to work. The critical factors are appropriate wound care and receiving the recommended prophylaxis on schedule.}{ref460}{Eat normally unless another medical condition requires a restriction. Do not postpone a vaccine dose because of a meal or fast.}
\indianentry{IND-072}{The old ``14 injections in the stomach'' are still required after dog bites.}{False}{Modern rabies vaccines are given by an approved intramuscular or intradermal schedule, not a course of fourteen abdominal injections. The schedule depends on prior vaccination and local guidance.}{ref459,ref460}{Seek care immediately after a possible exposure and keep every scheduled appointment. Some exposures also require antibody treatment around the wound.}
\indianentry{IND-073}{A child with measles should not be bathed because the rash will go back inside the body.}{False}{Bathing cannot drive the measles rash into the body. Gentle washing can be used for comfort and hygiene. The important risks include dehydration, pneumonia, and other complications.}{ref75}{Call the healthcare facility before attending because measles spreads readily through the air. Seek urgent care for breathing difficulty, poor drinking, or unusual drowsiness.}
\indianentry{IND-074}{Measles is a visitation of Sheetala Mata and should not be medically treated.}{False}{Measles is caused by a virus. A family may attach spiritual meaning to the illness, but a child with measles still needs medical guidance and monitoring for complications.}{ref75}{Contact a clinician promptly and call ahead. Follow advice about fluids, vitamin A treatment, and limiting exposure to others.}
\indianentry{IND-075}{Chickenpox is simply a manifestation of a goddess, so medicines should always be avoided.}{False}{Chickenpox is caused by varicella-zoster virus. Many children recover with supportive care; some patients have higher risks and may benefit from early antiviral treatment.}{ref76}{Seek early advice for an exposed or ill newborn, pregnant person, or someone with a weakened immune system. Do not give aspirin to a child with chickenpox.}
\indianentry{IND-076}{Smallpox was caused by divine displeasure.}{False}{Smallpox was caused by variola virus. Its spread between people and the success of surveillance and vaccination explain how the disease was controlled and ultimately eradicated.}{ref461}{Accounts of smallpox can acknowledge its religious history while identifying the infection and the public health measures responsible for eradication.}
\indianentry{IND-077}{Food restrictions alone can cure jaundice.}{False}{Jaundice is yellowing caused by an accumulation of bilirubin. Liver inflammation, obstruction of bile flow, and increased red blood cell breakdown are among its causes. Avoiding particular foods does not resolve all of them.}{ref458}{Seek prompt assessment for new yellowing of the eyes or skin so that treatment can address the cause.}
\indianentry{IND-078}{A person with jaundice should eat only bland or yellow-free food and avoid normal nutrition.}{False}{The yellow color of foods does not cause the yellowing of jaundice. Restricting someone to bland or colorless food has no general medical basis and may unnecessarily narrow their diet.}{ref458}{Follow dietary advice specific to the diagnosis. If eating or drinking becomes difficult, tell the treating clinician.}
\indianentry{IND-079}{Sugarcane juice cures jaundice.}{False}{Sugarcane juice provides fluid and sugar but has not been shown to lower bilirubin or cure the cause of jaundice. Its safety also depends on the water, equipment, and handling used to prepare it.}{ref458,ref451}{Arrange medical assessment for jaundice. Choose safely prepared drinks and account for the sugar if managing diabetes.}
\indianentry{IND-080}{A traditional healer can remove jaundice by transferring it into water, leaves, or another object.}{False}{Bilirubin cannot be transferred from a person's bloodstream into leaves or a bowl of water by a ritual. A color change in an object does not measure the patient's bilirubin level.}{ref458}{Use examination and appropriate testing to follow jaundice. A ritual result cannot rule out obstruction or other illness requiring treatment.}
\indianentry{IND-081}{Muslims have more children than Hindus.}{Uncertain}{In India's 2019--2021 National Family Health Survey, estimated total fertility was 2.36 births per woman among Muslims and 1.94 among Hindus. These are national period estimates based on age-specific birth rates. They neither describe every family nor establish why the groups differed.}{ref462}{Specify the country, survey period, and measure when citing this comparison. It cannot predict an individual's family size or establish that religion caused the difference.}
\indianentry{IND-082}{Poverty breeds more children.}{False}{India's National Family Health Survey found higher average fertility in lower household wealth quintiles. A household asset index is one measure of relative wealth; this observation cannot establish that poverty alone causes a family to have more children.}{ref462}{Interpret the group pattern alongside education, place of residence, and access to voluntary contraception. Avoid inferring an individual family's preferences from its income.}
\indianentry{IND-083}{The last generation lived longer because their food was biologically unmodified, their air was fresh, and their water was clean.}{False}{Indian life expectancy at birth has risen over the long term. Lower infant and child mortality contributes to this change; life expectancy at birth is different from remaining life expectancy for someone who has already reached adulthood. A few long-lived relatives cannot establish a generational average or its cause.}{ref463}{Compare the same population, ages, and time periods before attributing a change in longevity to particular foods, air quality, or drinking water.}
\end{enumerate}
