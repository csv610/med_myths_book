# Medical Myths Review Checklist

Use this checklist for every entry before publication. Record the result in an editorial review log; do not mark an entry complete based only on a search result or a general website.

## Claim and status

- [ ] The myth is a recognizable, commonly repeated health claim.
- [ ] The wording presents one clear claim and does not combine unrelated claims.
- [ ] The status is exactly one of `False`, `True`, or `Uncertain`.
- [ ] The status matches the strength and limits of the evidence.
- [ ] Absolute words such as “always,” “never,” “cures,” and “guarantees” are justified or explained.

## Fact and safety

- [ ] The fact directly answers the myth in the first sentence.
- [ ] The explanation is specific to this myth, scientifically accurate, and written in original language.
- [ ] Important exceptions, subgroups, or context are included.
- [ ] The entry does not encourage starting, stopping, or changing treatment without professional advice.
- [ ] Red-flag symptoms and urgent-care advice are included where delay could cause harm.
- [ ] Advice is appropriate for children, pregnancy, older adults, and people with chronic disease when relevant.

## Evidence

- [ ] Every important medical claim has a supporting citation.
- [ ] The citation directly supports the claim, not merely the general topic.
- [ ] High-risk claims use current guidelines, systematic reviews, government health agencies, or professional societies.
- [ ] Conflicting evidence is acknowledged and the uncertainty is accurately described.
- [ ] Reference details are complete, stable, and checked against the original source.
- [ ] The source date and access date are recorded for web sources.

## Editorial quality

- [ ] The entry is not a duplicate or near-duplicate of another entry.
- [ ] Medical terminology is explained for a general reader.
- [ ] Spelling, punctuation, units, drug names, and capitalization are correct.
- [ ] The entry does not imply that a population-level finding predicts an individual outcome.
- [ ] The entry has passed both medical review and copy-editing review.

## Publication gate

An entry is publishable only when all safety and evidence checks are complete and a qualified medical reviewer has approved it. Unreviewed entries should remain marked “pending review” in the editorial log.
